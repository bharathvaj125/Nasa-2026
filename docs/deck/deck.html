The challenge

Students can't feel the trade-offs of a space mission

  • A better instrument adds mass, power, data and cost, and each changes the rocket, the orbit and the radio.
  • Tools teach one topic at a time: orbits or rockets or spacecraft. Never the whole system.
  • So decisions stay abstract, and students rarely see a mission fail and learn why.

Our answer: a game where every decision has a consequence you can measure, simulate and discuss.

Real physics · Validity

Everything is coupled, and checked

  • Rocket equation sizes propellant from the delta-v budget; launch capacity is checked against the orbit.
  • Link budget in dB from distance, dish, band, power and pointing.
  • Eclipse, battery and ground contacts from propagated orbits.
  • 300 simulated flights with failures, storms and cost growth: the score is the average, not luck.
  • Unit-tested against textbook values.
Open source: src/engine in the repository4
Relevance · NASA data and methods

NASA is built into the game

  • NASA DONKI (live): recent solar eruptions set solar activity, which drives drag and radiation storms in every flight.
  • JPL Concept Maturity Levels & Mission Design Lab structure the four design steps.
  • Four mission segments from NASA's systems-engineering guide.
  • GMAT export to take your orbit into NASA's analysis tool.
  • Reference missions: Landsat 9, LRO, MRO, JWST.
Earth imagery: NASA Blue Marble (public domain)5
Collaboration · up to 4 players

One mission, four roles

  • Mission Lead, Launch & Ops, Spacecraft Engineer, Science & Comms: each controls different decisions.
  • Live sync, chat, and requests between roles: the crew must negotiate.
  • Each role has a private priority; the team score rewards meeting them together.
  • Only the lead gives the final go for launch.

Impact · Creativity

Learn why a mission succeeds or fails

  • The debrief explains each shortfall in numbers and suggests the fix.
  • "Why does this matter?" cards teach the rocket equation, link budget and margins in plain words.
  • Make the call: a solar storm hits mid-flight and your decision changes the outcome.
  • Save, compare and share designs. Re-fly with new random events.
  • Free, in any browser, for classrooms and clubs.
